Positioning against Calendly, honestly

Calendly owns scheduling. Everything else gets compared to it. So instead of pretending Timetics beats it at everything, the pages say who Timetics is for, and who it isn't.

At a glance

  • Three comparison pages: vs Calendly, vs Trafft, and Appointlet vs Calendly vs Timetics.
  • Gave away the enterprise crowd on purpose, to win WordPress and small business buyers.
  • Built on keyword data showing real buying intent behind small business booking searches.

01 / 04

The problem

When WordPress site owners look for booking tools, Calendly is the yardstick. Timetics had to show up in those comparisons.

But Timetics isn't built for big multi-team scheduling. Going head to head at that scale would cost trust. The copy had to admit Calendly's strengths, then claim a corner where Timetics wins.

02 / 04

The plan

  • Timetics vs Calendly: opens by crediting Calendly as the pioneer, then says it plainly: "If you're looking to scale your business with a GIANT solution, then Timetics is not for you." Letting that segment go earns trust with the one that fits.
  • Timetics vs Trafft: feature by feature. Trafft charges $29–$250 a month, Calendly $10–$20 per seat a month. That left room for Timetics as the WordPress-native, budget-friendly pick.
  • Appointlet vs Calendly vs Timetics: a three-way comparison for people actively shopping around.
03 / 04

What I did

  • Keyword research on volume, intent and difficulty for scheduling comparisons.
  • An outline for the three-way piece, built around buying decisions, not feature lists.
  • The full three-way blog draft.
  • Landing page copy for Timetics vs Calendly and Timetics vs Trafft.
  • A feature comparison list to back up the Trafft page.
