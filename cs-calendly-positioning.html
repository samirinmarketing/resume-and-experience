Positioning Against the Category Leader

Keyword research through comparison-page copywriting for Timetics vs. Calendly, Timetics vs. Trafft, and a three-way Appointlet vs. Calendly vs. Timetics piece.

The Problem 01 / 04

Calendly owns the scheduling category. When WordPress site owners search for booking solutions, "Calendly" is the reference point; everything else is measured against it. Timetics needed to appear in those comparison searches without pretending to be a Calendly replacement for everyone.

The harder problem: Timetics genuinely isn't for enterprise teams running complex, multi-org scheduling. Positioning that tries to compete head-on with Calendly at scale would lose credibility. The copy needed to concede Calendly's strengths, then carve out a specific segment where Timetics wins.

The keyword landscape reinforced this: "appointment booking" pulled 5,000 monthly searches at competition index 100, "scheduling app" had 5,000 at index 52, and "booking system for small business" had 500 searches but top-of-page bids of $10.48-$45.36, signalling serious commercial intent. The database also captured the misspelling "scheudling" at 5,000 monthly searches with low competition, a genuine long-tail opportunity.

Keyword data from Timetics AI Keyword Research Database.xlsx (Google Keyword Planner, Jan-Dec 2023, US market).

Strategy 02 / 04

Ran a full keyword research to content pipeline for three comparison angles:

  • Timetics vs. Calendly: Led with deliberate segment disqualification. The published copy opens by acknowledging Calendly as the pioneer, then explicitly states: "If you're looking to scale your business with a GIANT solution, then Timetics is not for you." This concedes the enterprise segment to earn trust with the WordPress/SMB segment where Timetics genuinely fits.
  • Timetics vs. Trafft: A feature-level comparison targeting users evaluating mid-tier alternatives. Trafft's pricing ($29-$250/month) versus Calendly's per-seat model ($10-$20/seat/month) created a natural three-way positioning map where Timetics could claim the WordPress-native, cost-effective position.
  • Appointlet vs. Calendly vs. Timetics: Full three-way comparison built from a separate keyword research and content outline, targeting comparison-shopping queries.

Execution 03 / 04

  • Keyword research document mapping search volume, intent, and difficulty for scheduling-comparison queries.
  • Content outline for the three-way comparison piece, structured around buyer decision factors rather than feature lists.
  • Full blog draft for the Appointlet vs. Calendly vs. Timetics comparison.
  • Webpage copy for both Timetics vs. Calendly and Timetics vs. Trafft, each as standalone landing pages.
  • A separate feature comparison list document supporting the Trafft piece with granular capability matching.
