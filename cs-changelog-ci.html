The problem

Timetics was up against much bigger names, and scheduling rivals charged premium prices: Calendly $10–$20 per seat a month, Trafft $29–$250 a month, YouCanBook.me $14.95 per user a month.

The team had strong opinions, but no system for tracking what rivals shipped, how fast, or what users cared about. Positioning ran on gut feel.

02 / 04

The plan

A documented five-step process:

  • List rivals in events (The Events Calendar, Events Manager, WP Event Manager, Event Tickets) and scheduling (Calendly, Trafft, Appointlet).
  • Pull their WordPress.org and CodeCanyon pages. Record installs and reviews as a baseline.
  • Log every changelog entry by date as New, Tweak or Fix.
  • Match social announcements to changelog dates. What did they shout about, and what did they ship quietly?
  • Compare feature speed: who ships what, how often, and what that means for the Timetics roadmap.

Two extra channels

  • Paid funnel teardown: every Facebook ad from The Events Calendar, sorted by funnel stage, with reactions and copy.
  • Social audit: Bookly's posts from Dec 2022 to Mar 2023, plus their comment sections. Complaints like blank pages after an update and unanswered support emails are positioning gold.
03 / 04

What I did

  • One reference doc, so anyone could run it without me.
  • Five event plugins benchmarked on installs and reviews.
  • Scheduling rivals tracked separately, since their buyers are different.
  • The output fed the comparison pages in September and October 2023.
